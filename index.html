Meg White is an engineer, researcher and creative technologist working across intelligent systems, electrical engineering, human-AI interaction and emerging technology. Her research is concerned with how increasingly autonomous systems affect human agency and safety, decision-making and the distribution of power, particularly when those systems are deployed in complex technical and social environments.

Her doctoral work focuses on machine learning for power-system operation, including topology identification, representation learning and the transfer of learned system information into control tasks. More broadly, she focuses on secure electrification, cyber-physical systems, adversarial engineering, trustworthy AI and practical methods for testing whether intelligent systems can and will behave as intended.

Alongside her academic work, Meaghan runs SLOBBERDOG, an independent R&D studio where she consults across technical innovation and workflows, and develops prototypes, interfaces and public experiments. Her practice combines research, fabrication, electronics, AI and critical inquiry with an emphasis on building tangible systems that expose assumptions, redistribute capability and give people greater control over the technologies around them.
